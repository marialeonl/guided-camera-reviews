01.

The problem

01.

From “having video” to “having good video”

Buyers could already add a video to a review, but only through a very basic camera that gave no help.

Context

Reviews help buyers decide with real experiences. Social commerce leaders had stopped treating the review as a form and turned it into native, incentivised video content. Until then, the review flow simply opened the phone’s own camera to take a photo or record a video. Nothing was guided or standardised, and the experience was inconsistent across iOS and Android.

Challenge

For buyers: “I don’t know what to record.” Videos missed the product details that matter, or buyers gave up.

For the business: review videos were a valuable but underused asset that could feed product pages and short-video content.

Image · Previous experienceThe review form + the phone’s default camera, annotated with pain points (deck style, blue callouts).

How might we help buyers record a useful product video with little effort, without lowering review completion?

?

“I don’t know what to show”

Wants to help but needs guidance on what to record. Benefits most from step-by-step prompts.

→

The fast reviewer

Knows what to say and values speed. Needs a short, simple flow.

★

The creator

Enjoys making detailed videos and is motivated by rewards. Produces the most reusable content.

04.

Key decisions

04.

From the endgame to priorities

We listed every feature from the endgame and ranked it by impact on getting good videos against effort. We went first for the causes of drop-off, and placed everything else around them.

Explains · Release 1 and Release 2

Guide with what people already say

The problem. An empty camera gives no hint of what to show or say.

What we decided. Prompts that change with the product in the first release. In the second, stickers built from what buyers already say: we took the comments users had left on the platform and analysed them with AI to find what they mention most.

Why. Guidance that sounds like the buyer’s own words is easier to follow and feels less like a form.

Explains · Release 2

Prevent instead of correct

The problem. Buyers found out about a dark or empty video only after recording and waiting.

What we decided. Flag it while recording, and improve light, audio and framing automatically, so nothing depends on the buyer fixing it.

Why. An error caught before sending costs a few seconds. One caught after costs the whole video.

1

Guidance vs. speed

Option AA full guided storyboard with an editor, like the quality-first benchmark.
We choseThree short takes with one prompt each, and no editor in the first version.

Why: guidance had to help the buyer who doesn’t know what to show, without slowing down the buyer in a hurry or lowering review completion.

2

Separate modes by intent

Option AOne camera that mixes guided capture, free recording and gallery uploads.
We choseThree tabs. Guided video records in-app only; free video and photo also accept the gallery.

Why: guided takes only work if they are recorded with the prompts. Buyers with an existing video still have a fast path.

3

Designing for interruptions

A call, a locked screen or the app going to background should never cost the buyer their work. I defined what each mode keeps:

SituationFree videoGuided videoPhoto
Interruption while recordingPauses and keeps everything recordedDrops only the take in progress; completed takes stayReturns ready to shoot
Back from previewKeeps the video and recording stateKeeps every take and stepStarts fresh
DiscardAsks for confirmationAsks for confirmationNo confirmation: easy to retake

Why: the cost of losing content grows with the effort it took. Confirmation and persistence follow that cost.

What we did differently

We took guided capture from the quality model, and kept the flow short for buyers in a hurry.

  1. 1

    Our own camera inside the review flow. It replaced the phone’s default camera with one consistent experience on iOS and Android.

  2. 2

    A guided mode. Three short takes, each with an on-screen prompt: the full product, the details, the product in use.

  3. 3

    Three modes for three profiles. Guided video, free video and photo, in one camera.

  4. 4

    Automatic quality. Audio clean-up, stabilisation and framing, and auto trim apply on their own. Every video gets better with no extra step for the buyer.

  5. 5

    Stickers from real reviews. We analysed the comments buyers write most often and turned them into stickers, so they can say what matters in one tap.

  6. 6

    Ship in phases. A basic camera first, to learn fast. Then, release by release, quick helps that improve videos with no extra effort for the buyer.

What we learned as a team

What worked

01

Ship basic, then help

A simple first release gave us real videos to learn from, and a clear base for the quick helps.

02

Learn from real videos

After launch, we reviewed a sample of published videos to see what buyers run into most: a static camera, shaky footage or low light. The second release adds quick, automatic helps for those moments. TBC: confirm the analysis helped prioritise the helps

✦ AI-native

Video review with AI. AI helped us classify the sample of published videos by situation, so we could see patterns at scale. TBC

03

Listen to what buyers already say

We took the comments buyers had already left and analysed them with AI to find what they mention most. That became the stickers of the second release.
