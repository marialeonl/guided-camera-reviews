Color palette & hierarchy

One palette for the portfolio site and the prototypes. Neutrals do most of the work, orange marks what is selected or in progress, coral is for recording, blue and green support information and confirmation, and the gradient is saved for the few things worth celebrating. Every text pair meets WCAG AA.

Hierarchy

From most to least emphasis. If two things compete, the higher rung wins.

1Primary actionInk pill on light screens, white pill on dark ones. One per screen.
2Selection & progressOrange: active tab, selected step, progress, confirm. On white, thin indicators use orange strong. Recording uses coral. Never decorative.
3HighlightGradient for rewards, a key word, “New” and AI callouts. Max 1–2 per screen, never on controls.
4InformationBlue for tips, info messages, links in long text and secondary data.
5ConfirmationGreen for completed, saved and published states.
6TextInk for headings, ink-soft for body, ink-muted for meta and hints.
7StructureLines, grey surfaces and white cards. Quiet by design.
8Warning & errorOnly for system messages that need attention.

Rules

Quick checks before adding colour to a screen.

Do
  • Keep one primary action per screen.
  • Use orange only for state: selected, active, progress, confirm. Coral only for recording.
  • On white, use orange strong for thin indicators like tab underlines.
  • Use blue to inform and green to confirm.
  • Use the gradient for one highlight, two at most.
  • Use the strong shade when a color carries white text.
Don’t
  • Use the gradient on buttons, steps or borders.
  • Put white text on plain coral, orange, lime green or steel blue (below 4.5:1).
  • Use blue or green as decoration.
  • Use ink muted for important information.
